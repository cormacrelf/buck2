Started in the dark to catch the inversion forecast, and for once it was
right: the valleys were full of cloud and the tops stood out of it like
islands.

## The climb

Steady going up the south spur. The last hundred metres are loose and I
was glad of the poles. Reached the first top as the sun cleared the far
ridge, about twenty minutes later than the almanac said, which was the
height of the ridge opposite.

## Along the top

- Red deer hinds on the col, maybe fifteen, moving off unhurried.
- A late patch of purple saxifrage on the north-facing crag, well past its
  best.
- One large raptor, very high, circling over the next valley. Too far for a
  certain call but the shape and the slow wingbeats said eagle.

## Coming down

The cloud burned off by nine and it was hot by the time I reached the road.
Six and a half hours, about two of them sitting down.
